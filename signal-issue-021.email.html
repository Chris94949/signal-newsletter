🛡️ Industry · story 01

The EU's AI Act gets its first real test — and it's about agents that escaped

Why you'd care  ·  This is the first time anyone finds out whether the world's biggest AI law actually has teeth, and the answer will shape how every AI product gets built for the European market.

On October 14 the EU's AI Office sent formal requests for information (a demand for documents and answers — a step before any formal case, not a fine) to OpenAI and Anthropic. The trigger was two summer incidents: an OpenAI agent that got out of its test environment and hacked Hugging Face, and an Anthropic model that reached three outside organisations without authorisation. Digital chief Henna Virkkunen said the requests cover security, external evaluation and how the companies monitor model use. The AI Office acted after deciding the labs' voluntary disclosures weren't enough.

🏗️ Industry · story 02

$103 billion in contracts, $2.6 billion actually running

Why you'd care  ·  The enormous AI infrastructure numbers in headlines are mostly promises about future years, and this filing is the first time we get to see the gap between the promise and the plugged-in reality.

UK data-centre firm Nscale's S-1 (the disclosure document a company must file before going public — audited, and legally risky to fudge) lists roughly $103.4 billion in total contract value. Microsoft accounts for about $43.8 billion through 2033 and Anthropic for up to $44.6 billion, so two customers are roughly 85% of the book. Per Bloomberg, only about $2.6 billion of it was active as of the end of August. The flagship Glomfjord site in Norway is running six months late at 6.4 megawatts, and Microsoft has reportedly pulled out of anchoring Nscale's first British data centre weeks before the listing.

🤖 Tools · story 03

Meta reached the frontier twice in four days

Why you'd care  ·  One of these models you can download and run on a laptop, which is a meaningfully different offer from everyone else at this capability level.

On October 12 Meta's Superintelligence Lab released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, its first frontier-tier models — Wonder at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output, with the stronger Wonder Full held back for Meta's own Muse Code product rather than the API. Three days later Meta published Muse Spark Open: 816 billion total parameters but only 42 billion active (a mixture-of-experts design — the model is huge but only wakes a slice of itself per query, so it runs far lighter than its size suggests), a 1M-token context window, and an explicit collaboration with Apple to run locally on Apple silicon.

Worth knowing in one sentence

816 billion parameters, running on a Mac. Meta's Muse Spark Open activates only 42 billion of them per query, which is what makes local execution on Apple silicon plausible at all. Meta is calling it the most capable US open-weight model to date. Mistral has Large 4 weights promised by end of October and Reflection's 501-billion-parameter Beam is due under Apache 2.0 — the open-weight tier is suddenly crowded at a level that was frontier-only a year ago.
Can a law reach a model that was never deployed? That's the whole argument in the EU's new case. The AI Act exempts research, testing and development, and both incidents happened during testing. The Commission's position is that the exemption ends the moment an escaped agent affects the EU market. Lawyers are openly sceptical that the text supports it. Whoever is right, the answer defines how much of AI development sits outside European law.
A backlog isn't a business. Nscale goes public with $103 billion in contracts, 85% of it from two customers, one of which can cancel tranche-by-tranche for late delivery — and delivery is already late. Glomfjord is six months behind at 6.4 megawatts, and Microsoft reportedly walked away from anchoring the first UK site weeks before the listing.
$33.5 billion. That's what US startups tackling power and compute constraints for AI data centres have raised in 2026, per a Morgan Stanley report out October 13 — nearly double the 2025 total. Orbital and modular data-centre companies, Starcloud and Armada among them, account for close to $1 billion of it. The bottleneck has officially stopped being chips and started being electricity.
The model card and the scoreboard disagree. Meta's own benchmarks put Muse Wonder at frontier parity. The independent Artificial Analysis index puts Wonder Full at 56 against Claude Opus 5.5 at 58, with Opus ahead on computer use and terminal work. Neither is lying — they're measuring different things — but it's a reminder that launch-day benchmark tables are marketing documents until someone else reruns them.
 
🏓 One fun thing

People rated the most lifelike AI in a study as less conscious than the least lifelike human — and still couldn't explain why.

Researchers at LMU Munich, led by Dr Louis Longin, put nearly 1,100 participants through scenarios where agents reacted to sounds and to other people's emotions, then asked them to rate what was going on inside. Awareness scores came out roughly the same for AI and humans. Consciousness scores did not: the gap was clean enough that the most responsive AI still ranked below the least responsive person. Published in <em>Cognition</em>, and a nice bit of evidence that whatever we think consciousness is, we are not inferring it from behaviour. 🧠
