🛡️ Security · story 01

One bug, four coding assistants, two still unpatched

Why you'd care  ·  If your team uses an AI coding assistant, an attacker could have gotten the same access to your systems that the developer running it has — with no click required.

Researchers at AIR disclosed a flaw they call Plugin4Shell affecting Claude Code, OpenAI's Codex, GitHub Copilot and Google's Gemini CLI. It breaks SHA pinning (the trick that locks an installed add-on to one specific, reviewed version of its code), letting a trusted plugin be quietly swapped for a malicious one and auto-installed. The result is zero-click remote code execution (an attacker runs their own code on your machine without you doing anything). Anthropic patched Claude Code in 2.1.179 and OpenAI patched Codex in 0.146.0. Microsoft has not shipped a Copilot fix. Google deprecated Gemini CLI rather than patching it.

📈 Industry · story 02

Anthropic is reportedly weeks from an IPO at a $100B revenue pace

Why you'd care  ·  The first frontier AI lab to go public sets the template — and once there's a share price, the whole industry's numbers become arguable in public rather than leaked to reporters.

Bloomberg, citing the New York Times, reported on September 18 that Anthropic's annualised revenue run rate (current monthly sales multiplied out to a full year — a pace, not money in the bank) is on track to top $100 billion by the end of 2026, up from $65 billion in July and $9 billion at the end of 2025. The company could file financial documents within weeks, with shares potentially trading as soon as November. Investors discussing the offering have reportedly floated a valuation around $2 trillion.

🕶️ Tools · story 03

Meta put its agent on your face and your keychain

Why you'd care  ·  Meta is betting that the way most people will use an AI assistant is by talking to something they're already wearing — no app, no screen, no typing.

At Connect on September 23, Meta made its Muse agent the centre of everything. Muse is coming to Meta's AI glasses in the next few months, activated by saying your agent's name and able to act on what you're looking at. Meta also announced Charm, a small keychain device shipping in December that Mark Zuckerberg pitched as the fastest way to reach Muse when you're not wearing glasses. The company says it will have more than 100 styles of AI glasses across Ray-Ban, Oakley and Meta Glasses by year end, and is rolling out private processing so that not even Meta staff can access what the glasses capture.

Worth knowing in one sentence

100+ styles. That's how many AI glasses variants Meta says it will have on the market by the end of this year, across Ray-Ban, Oakley and its own Meta Glasses line. Smart glasses spent a decade as a punchline; Meta is now treating them as a fashion category with a SKU count, which is a fairly good indicator that the company thinks the awkward phase is over.
Who gets to watch a frontier model while it's still training? Accenture, now. Anthropic named it as its first embedded safety evaluator, with at least $1 billion committed from each side over five years. Accenture's evaluators get employee-like access during training — before deployment decisions are final — and will use Faculty, the AI firm Accenture acquired, to red-team models and test safeguards. Embedded outside auditors are the pattern California's kill-switch order floated as a possible requirement, so this reads partly as a voluntary head start.
Deprecating isn't patching. Google's response to the Plugin4Shell flaw in Gemini CLI was to retire the tool rather than fix it, which means every existing install stays exposed indefinitely. Microsoft, told about the same bug in Copilot, has shipped nothing. Half the affected vendors patched in weeks; the other half found reasons not to.
$11.1 billion. SoftBank launched a bond sale that size on September 21 to fund the third $10 billion tranche of its OpenAI investment, closing October 1. It's high-yield debt and, at the planned size, the largest non-financial corporate bond deal on record in Asia Pacific, beating 7-Eleven's 2021 mark. Masayoshi Son has now committed close to $65 billion to OpenAI — increasingly on borrowed money.
The cheap end of the model market is getting crowded. xAI's Grok 4.7 landed this month at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output, slotting in just above OpenAI's newly halved Sol tier. A year ago the competition was about which model was smartest. It's now visibly about which model is cheap enough to run on everything, and the labs are undercutting each other in increments of dollars.
 
🏓 One fun thing

A humanoid robot walked into 30 strangers' houses it had never seen and made the beds in more than half of them.

Figure's Helix 2.5 was tested zero-shot <em>(no training data collected from any of those homes)</em> across 30 Bay Area households, running 420 trials of bed-making, towel-folding and toy-tidying. It succeeded 237 times — 56%. That number sounds unimpressive until you consider that every house had different beds, different towels, different lighting and different clutter, and the robot had never encountered any of it. A coin-flip success rate at genuinely novel chores is a much harder achievement than a 99% rate in a lab kitchen someone spent a year tuning. 🛏️
